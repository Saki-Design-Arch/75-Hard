# Social Media Strategy — Saki Fitness / Date Miles Club

**Owner:** Saki · Former personal trainer
**Channels:** TikTok [@saki.fitness247](https://www.tiktok.com/@saki.fitness247) · Date Miles Club (Facebook group, hybrid in-person/online run & walk club)
**Priorities (in order):** 1) Grow Date Miles Club  2) Build personal training clients  3) TikTok itself is a means to those two ends, not a separate goal

---

## 1. The core idea: one funnel, three layers

Right now TikTok and Date Miles Club are two disconnected things. They should be one funnel:

```
   TikTok (@saki.fitness247)
   "the storefront window"
   Short-form video → reach strangers, prove you know your stuff
              │
              ▼
   Date Miles Club (Facebook group)
   "the clubhouse"
   Hybrid run/walk community → turns strangers into regulars
              │
              ▼
   Personal training / coaching
   "the paid layer"
   Regulars who trust you → become paying clients
```

Every piece of content should be doing one of two jobs: **pulling new people from TikTok into Date Miles Club**, or **turning Date Miles Club regulars into coaching leads**. If a video doesn't do either, it's just noise — fine occasionally for reach, but not the backbone.

This also means: don't over-index on "building a personal brand" for its own sake. TikTok's job is distribution for the club and the coaching business, not a separate creator career — that changes what "winning" looks like (club members and client inquiries, not just follower count).

---

## 2. Brand foundation (do this first — takes under an hour)

**TikTok bio**, rewrite to do three things in three lines — who you are, what the club is, and a clear next step:

```
🏃‍♀️ Former personal trainer
🏃 Founder, Date Miles Club — [Your City] run/walk crew, all paces
👇 Join the club or ask about coaching
[link-in-bio URL]
```

Use a free link-in-bio tool (Linktree, Beacons, or even a single Google Form) with exactly two buttons: **"Join Date Miles Club"** (→ Facebook group link) and **"Ask about coaching"** (→ DM prompt or a short inquiry form). Two choices, not ten — don't bury the CTA in a wall of links.

**Date Miles Club Facebook group**, add a pinned welcome post at the top with: what the club is, the standing meetup schedule (day/time/location), an all-paces/no-drop policy statement, and a link to your TikTok. This single post will answer 80% of the questions new members ask you personally.

**Liability note (worth the 20 minutes):** you're a former trainer publicly leading group runs/walks and giving fitness advice. Add a short disclaimer to your TikTok bio or a pinned video ("content is for informational purposes, not medical advice, consult a doctor before starting a new program") and use a simple digital waiver (a free Google Form works) for in-person meetups. Cheap insurance against a real problem later.

**Consistency:** use the same profile photo and a consistent name ("Date Miles Club") across TikTok content, the Facebook group, and any future Instagram/Strava presence, so people recognize it's the same thing everywhere.

## 3. TikTok content pillars

Weighted toward what actually serves your two real goals — club growth and coaching credibility — not generic entertainment for its own sake.

| Pillar | Weight | Purpose | Example hooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Date Miles Club** — recaps, invites, member spotlights | 35% | Direct fuel for club growth; social proof | "We had 14 people show up to run 3 miles and make friends. Here's who's coming next Saturday." / "POV: you joined a run club to get fit and left with your whole new friend group." |
| **Coach Saki** — form checks, myths, training tips | 30% | Credibility that converts into coaching inquiries | "I was a personal trainer for [X] years. Here's the #1 mistake I saw every single week." / "Things your trainer wishes you knew but won't say to your face." |
| **The Journey** — your own 75 Hard / Foundation Protocol progress | 20% | Authentic proof-of-work; humanizes the coach | "Day 42 of 75 Hard and today almost broke me — here's what actually happened." / "I built my own tracker because every app out there was garbage — here's how I log every rep." |
| **Relatable / trend** — fitness culture humor, trend audio with a running/training twist | 15% | Keeps the algorithm feeding you to new people; pure how-to content plateaus without it | "Run club vs. actual bar: a comparison." / Trend audio + a training or running punchline. |

A few notes on execution:

- **Hook in the first 1–2 seconds.** Say the interesting thing immediately — no "hey guys" windups.
- **Always end with an explicit ask**, and rotate it: "comment MILES and I'll send you the link," "link in bio to join Saturday," "3 coaching spots open this month, DM me FIT." TikTok viewers don't infer what you want them to do — tell them.
- **Comment-keyword CTAs work double duty** on TikTok: they boost comments (which the algorithm rewards) *and* start a DM conversation with a warm lead. Use this often, especially for the Date Miles Club pillar.
- Since you're comfortable with any format, lead with **talking-head + voiceover B-roll** (you coaching, running, at meetups) — it consistently outperforms static photo/text on TikTok, and B-roll from actual meetups is content you're already generating just by showing up.

## 4. What Facebook (Date Miles Club) is actually for

TikTok is discovery; the Facebook group is where people become regulars. Use its native tools instead of just posting updates:

- **Create a Facebook Event for every single meetup**, even recurring ones. Events show up in members' notifications and Facebook's discovery surfaces in a way normal posts don't — it's free promotion you're currently not using.
- **Post 3–4x/week**, mixing: meetup recap (photos + who showed up), a discussion prompt ("what's one mile you're proud of this week?"), and a logistics/reminder post for the next meetup.
- **Ask a quick question in every recap post.** Groups grow on engagement, not just reach — a post that gets comments shows up higher in members' feeds than one that just gets likes.
- Cross-post your best TikToks into the group natively (not just a link) — members who joined from TikTok will recognize and engage with content they already like.

## 5. Weekly rhythm

| Day | TikTok | Facebook (Date Miles Club) |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Coach Saki tip | Discussion prompt / weekly mileage callout |
| Wed | The Journey (75 Hard day-count) | Reminder post for upcoming meetup |
| Fri | Relatable/trend | — |
| Sat/Sun | Date Miles Club recap (from that week's meetup) | Recap post (photos, shoutouts) + Event post for next meetup |

That's ~4 TikToks and ~4 Facebook posts a week — sustainable for one person, and every post has a job.

---

## 6. First 30 days (concrete, not theoretical)

**Week 1 — Foundation**
Set up the link-in-bio and rewritten TikTok bio. Post an origin-story video ("why I left personal training to build Date Miles Club" or "why I started this club"). Post your first Coach Saki tip. Create a Facebook Event for your next meetup and pin the welcome post in the group.

**Week 2 — Rhythm**
Start the weekly cadence above. Post your first meetup recap with real photos/clips. Begin the 75 Hard day-count content using your own tracker as the visual ("here's day X, here's how I'm logging it").

**Week 3 — Social proof**
Feature a member spotlight or testimonial from Date Miles Club (a quick clip of them talking about why they come, or a shoutout to someone's first 5K/most-miles-this-month). Push a comment-keyword CTA specifically to grow the Facebook group.

**Week 4 — Convert**
Run your first soft coaching pitch ("taking 3 spots for 1:1 coaching this month, DM me FIT"). Look at what actually got views/comments/joins across the month and double down on that pillar in month 2 — don't guess, follow the data.

---

## 7. What to watch (keep it to four numbers)

- **Date Miles Club membership growth** (the #1 goal — track weekly)
- **Meetup attendance** (the real-world health of the club — a growing group with flat attendance is a warning sign)
- **TikTok follower growth + average views per video** (is it working as a discovery engine)
- **Coaching DMs/inquiries → paying clients** (the actual business outcome)

Ignore likes as a primary metric — they don't move any of your three real goals.

---

## 8. Optional, high-leverage add-ons

- **Strava Club.** Free, and purpose-built for exactly what Date Miles Club does — logging miles, leaderboards, kudos, works equally well for the in-person and online members. Very low effort to set up, and it gives remote members a way to feel part of the club between meetups. Worth doing before Instagram.
- **Instagram (Reels).** Same video, reposted — near-zero extra effort since you're already filming for TikTok. Worth turning on once the TikTok/Facebook rhythm feels sustainable, not before.
- **The tracker itself as content.** This repo's 75 Hard tracker is a legitimate content asset — "I built my own tool because nothing else worked" is a differentiated hook most trainers can't use. If you ever want to open it up as something Date Miles Club members can use too, that's a natural phase-2 project, separate from this strategy.
