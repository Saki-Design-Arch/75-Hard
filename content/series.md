# Series

Ten series, each one pulling from a different category of fitness TikTok, so the account
covers the whole space instead of competing in one lane. Each has a day one pilot, all
dated 2026-09-13.

This is a pilot day, not a permanent slate. Ten series is too many to sustain and it is
supposed to be - the point is to shoot one of each, find out which ones he can actually
hold and which ones the audience answers, then cut to four. The kill rule is at the bottom.

## Templates

Each series has a fixed recurring shape - `scripts/templates/` holds one file per series
with that shape pre-built: the beats that repeat every episode, the on screen text
patterns, the series-specific rules, and brackets for what changes. The generic
`scripts/TEMPLATE.md` is the fallback for a one-off that isn't part of any series (or for
the two reactive series below, which borrow their shape straight from `formats.md`
instead - Answer Cut and Honest Day respectively).

Writing episode 2 or later of a series starts from its template, not from a blank page or
from the generic template - that's the whole point of building one per series.

## Two reactive series with no pilot

These cannot be faked into existence on day one and trying is the fastest way to look like
every other account.

**Answer Cut** - a real comment, read out loud, answered to camera. Needs comments. It goes
live the day the first real question arrives, and it will outperform most of the slate.

**The Honest Log** - the missed session, the bad weigh in, the day it fell apart. Needs a
failure that actually happened. Do not invent one. It will arrive on its own, and when it
does it becomes the most shareable thing on the account.

## What each series actually is

**1 · The Standard · Day X** - today's session, compressed, with the day counter climbing.
The spine of the account. Every other series can die and this one still carries it. The
counter itself is the retention device: people follow a number that is moving.

**2 · Zero Pull Ups** - one skill, tracked from zero to the first rep. Dead hang, scapular
pulls, negatives, inverted rows, band assist, then the rep. This is the highest ceiling
series on the slate because the payoff video is worth more than every episode that led to
it, and it only exists because those episodes exist.

**3 · The Mile** - a timed mile, same treadmill, same framing, same shoes, retested every
month. 11:48 is the number to beat. The arc runs to sub nine, then sub eight, then sub six.
Never change the frame or the comparison is worthless.

**4 · Date Miles Club** - the date sets the distance, up to three miles. Launches October 1
with one member. The calendar writes the hook 365 days a year, which makes this the only
series that can never run out of episodes. Full charter and rules in the club reference
file.

**5 · Eleven Numbers** - the scale plus the tape. Eleven measurements instead of one, on
camera, including the ones that did not move. This is the series that protects him from
quitting over a single bad weigh in, and it teaches the audience the same thing.

**6 · Big Guy Beginner** - every modification that makes training possible at 258 pounds.
Fists instead of flat palms. Incline push ups. Inverted rows instead of pull ups. Rowing
when the knees complain. Shallow dips. This is the most useful content on the account to
the person who needs it most, and almost nobody is making it.

**7 · Twenty One Hundred** - 2,100 to 2,250 calories, 180 to 200 g protein, at 258 pounds.
Real plates, real portions, no supplement pitch, no moralizing about food. The most
searchable series on the slate.

**8 · The Ruck Hour** - the loaded walk, long form, unpolished, talking while moving. The
one slot where the account is allowed to be slow, and the best B roll it produces. Light
pack now, 25 to 35 lb by Phase 3.

**9 · Marine Standard** - what the actual standards are, and where he is against them right
now. This series is the reason the account has a destination instead of a vibe. Hard rule:
Marine style conditioning, self coached. Never a claimed affiliation, never a claimed
service record.

**10 · Always Be Yourself** - straight to lens, one idea, quiet, no music swell. Ends with
the signature line word for word. This is the series that turns a viewer into someone who
follows a person rather than watching a clip.

## Pilot day shoot order

Grouped by setup so one outfit and one location cover several videos. This is the whole
skill of posting multiple times a day: never set up twice for one video.

**Setup A · Gym or bars, training clothes** - shoot in this order, it follows the session:
1 The Standard, 2 Zero Pull Ups, 6 Big Guy Beginner, 9 Marine Standard.

**Setup B · Treadmill** - 3 The Mile. Same framing you will use for every retest, so decide
it now and write it down.

**Setup C · Outside, pack on** - 8 The Ruck Hour. Golden hour if possible, it is the only
series where the light matters.

**Setup D · Kitchen** - 7 Twenty One Hundred. Shoot the whole day of food in one pass, even
the boring meals.

**Setup E · Plain wall, indoors, no gym in frame** - 10 Always Be Yourself, 4 Date Miles
Club. Both are straight to lens. Shoot 10 first while the voice is calm.

**Setup F · Scale and tape** - 5 Eleven Numbers. Already scripted. Morning, before food.

Realistic day: setups A, E, and F are one morning. B and D are the same afternoon. C is its
own hour. If only half of it gets filmed today, film A, E and F and push the rest to
tomorrow. Posting six real videos beats posting ten rushed ones.

## Posting order for the pilots

Do not dump ten videos in one day. Post three today and stagger the rest across the week
while they are still day one footage:

| Post | Series | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| Today, morning | 5 Eleven Numbers | The weigh in and the tape. The most honest thing on the account opens it |
| Today, evening | 1 The Standard, Day 1 | The spine starts, the counter starts |
| Tomorrow | 10 Always Be Yourself | Identity, while day one is still the subject |
| Then | 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9 | One a day, best footage first |
| 09-20 to 09-22 | The club arc | Three consecutive days, then October 1 |

## The kill rule

Two weeks after the pilots, keep four series and stop the rest. Four is what one person can
hold daily without the quality dropping, and a dropped series is worse than one that never
started.

The club is one of the four and it is not up for a vote - it is the offer the account
exists to open.

The other three get picked on evidence, not taste: watch time first, saves and shares
second, follows third. Likes decide nothing.

Whatever gets cut goes to `backlog.md` with a note on why, not deleted. A series that
failed in month one with 200 followers can work in month six with 20,000.
