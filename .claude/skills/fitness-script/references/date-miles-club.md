# Date Miles Club

The offer the account is being built to open. Written by Saki, never launched. This file
holds the charter as he wrote it, the math on it, and what has to change before it goes
out to people.

## The charter, in his words

> Welcome to Date Miles Club.
>
> This is not a challenge. It is a commitment.
>
> Every day the date sets the distance. If it is the 7th you move seven miles. Run or
> walk. The calendar decides.
>
> Days one through fifteen are solo. This is where discipline is built quietly.
>
> Day sixteen through the end of the month are team days. Same miles. Shared effort. In
> person or online. Nobody gets left behind.
>
> There are three tiers because effort matters more than ego. Complete it your way. One
> full session. Or continuous. Choose honestly. Respect every tier.
>
> You get two recovery passes per month. Any day can become one mile. Smart pacing keeps
> you in the game all year.
>
> Month six is a personal marathon. The last day of the year is a team marathon. Same
> rules. Same unity. Same finish.
>
> This community runs on a few principles. Consistency over speed. Celebrate effort.
> Lift each other. No comparison. No quitting.
>
> If you are here you are already in motion. Introduce yourself. Share your why. Then
> check the calendar and move the miles.
>
> The date is waiting. Starting 2026.

The writing is right. The structure is right. The solo half then team half is genuinely
smart, and it is the part that lets the club launch with one person in it. The principles
are the ABY voice without being told to be.

## Status, stated plainly

Written. Dated to start in 2026. Never stuck to. As of 2026-09-13 it has not launched
and has no members.

## The math

This is why it did not stick.

| Month length | Total miles | Solo days 1 to 15 | Team days 16 to end | Avg on team days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 28 days | 406 mi | 120 mi | 286 mi | 22.0 mi/day |
| 30 days | 465 mi | 120 mi | 345 mi | 23.0 mi/day |
| 31 days | 496 mi | 120 mi | 376 mi | 23.5 mi/day |

At a 3 mph walk, a 31 day month is 165 hours of moving. That is 5.3 hours a day,
every day, on top of a six day training program. The 31st alone is 10.3 hours. The 25th
is 8.3 hours.

496 miles a month is 112 miles a week. That is peak ultramarathon training volume, held
for twelve straight months.

The two recovery passes do not rescue it. Spent on the two biggest days of a 31 day
month they save 59 miles and leave 437.

The three tiers do not rescue it either, because they govern *how* the distance gets
completed - split through the day, one session, or continuous - not how far it is. Every
tier still owes the full date.

For scale: his own baseline at program start was 2,784 steps a day, about 1.4 miles, or
43 miles a month. The charter as written asks for eleven times that, starting on day one.

## The verdict

He did not fail the club. The club's arithmetic failed him, and it would fail every
person he invited into it. Publishing it as written would hurt people who trusted it,
and it breaks two ABY rules at once: minimal pressure, and no promises the plan cannot
keep.

That is not a reason to kill it. The concept is excellent - the calendar as the coach,
nobody deciding today whether today counts. It needs one number changed.

## The fix, three options

Every option keeps the charter intact: the date still sets the distance, solo first half,
team second half, two recovery passes, month six and the last day of the year.

**Option A · The ceiling.** The date sets the distance up to a ceiling. Below the
ceiling the calendar rules as written. At or above it, the day is the ceiling.

| Ceiling | Miles per month | Daily average | Hardest day |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 mi | 90 | 2.9 | 3 mi |
| 4 mi | 118 | 3.8 | 4 mi |
| 5 mi | 145 | 4.7 | 5 mi |
| 6 mi | 171 | 5.5 | 6 mi |
| 10 mi | 265 | 8.5 | 10 mi |

The ceiling can climb through the year, which turns the club into a progression instead
of a wall. Start the year at 3, add a mile each quarter.

**Option B · Digit sum.** The date decides, digits and all. The 25th is 2 plus 5, so
seven miles. The 31st is four miles. Hardest day in any month is the 29th at eleven
miles. 172 miles a month, 5.5 a day.

Keeps the calendar poetry completely intact and needs no ceiling rule. The cost is that
the number stops being obvious at a glance, and "if it is the 7th you move seven miles"
is the line that makes the club understandable in four seconds.

**Option C · Half the date.** Round up. The 31st is sixteen miles. 256 miles a month.
Still a 16 mile day and still too much for a first year. Listed for completeness, not
recommended.

**Recommendation: Option A with a ceiling of 3 miles for year one.** 90 miles a month,
2.9 a day. That is double his current baseline, it survives a six day training program,
and it is finishable by a 257 lb beginner and by whoever joins, which is the actual test.
The rule reads clean: *the date sets the distance, up to three miles.* Then the ceiling
climbs as the club gets stronger, and that climb is itself a year of content.

Month six personal marathon and the year end team marathon stay untouched. Those are
supposed to hurt.

## Launch plan

**Do not wait for January.** The club is date driven, so any first of the month works,
and the next one is **2026-10-01**. Eighteen days out from today. Waiting until January
2027 costs a quarter of a year to gain nothing.

Month one runs solo by design - the charter already says days one through fifteen are
solo, which means the club can launch with exactly one member and be running correctly.
Team days from the 16th run online with whoever is there. Three people is a team.

Sequence:

1. **Now to 2026-09-30** - post the arc below. Fix the ceiling. Set the rule in writing.
2. **2026-10-01** - day one, three miles, on camera. He is member number one.
3. **Month one** - solo half filmed daily. Team half open to anyone in the comments who
   asks. No link, no list, no ask - the account is under 1,000 followers.
4. **10,000 followers** - the list opens and the club becomes a real CTA.
5. **Month six, 2027-03** - personal marathon, filmed. This is a landmark video.
6. **2026-12-31 or the year end of the first full cycle** - team marathon.

## How it shows up in content

The club is not an announcement. It is a series with an ending.

**The three part opening arc, before launch:**

1. *I wrote this and never did it.* The charter on screen, read out loud, then the
   admission. This is the most honest thing on the account and the most shareable.
2. *The math is why.* 496 miles a month. Show the number. The failure was design, not
   character. This video teaches something real and nobody else on fitness TikTok is
   doing the arithmetic out loud.
3. *Here is the fixed version and the date it starts.* The ceiling, the rule, October 1.

**Then, ongoing:** every club day is a Process post with the date as the hook. The 14th
is fourteen. The calendar writes the hook for free, 365 days a year, which is the most
valuable property this club has.

**CTA by stage:** none under 1,000 followers - he is doing it alone and saying so. From
10,000, the club is the only ask on the account. See `growth-ladder.md`.

## Rules for writing about it

Never state a mileage rule that is not the current written rule. If the ceiling changes,
this file changes first.

Never invite someone into a volume that has not been checked. Every time the rule
changes, the month total gets recalculated and written here.

The club is a commitment, not a challenge - his word, and it is the right one. No
countdown urgency, no scarcity, no shame for anyone who takes a recovery pass. The
recovery passes are part of the design, not a failure state.
