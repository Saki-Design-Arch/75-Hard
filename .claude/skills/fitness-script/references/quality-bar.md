# Quality bar

## Retention mechanics

- **First frame** carries readable text. Assume sound off and a thumb already moving.
- **No slack.** Every clip cuts one beat earlier than comfortable. Dead air at second
  four kills the whole video.
- **One idea.** A second idea is a second video, and now the week is easier.
- **Movement in frame** in the first second: a rep starting, a walk, a reveal. Static
  talking heads need a much stronger first line to survive.
- **Loop the close into the open** where the format allows it - it buys a second watch
  without a gimmick.
- **Captions burned in.** Most of the audience is watching silently.
- **Real audio under the voice.** Breathing, plates, feet on a treadmill. It is proof.
- **Length follows content.** 15 seconds well cut beats 40 padded. Only the ruck and
  chapter marks earn long.

## Voice check, every script

Read the draft imagining one person alone at night. Then check:

- Does it speak from the journey rather than above it?
- Does it show process, not only results?
- Is there something to do today?
- Does it leave hope rather than pressure?
- Does it sound like one conversation, not a broadcast?
- If it is motivational or identity, does it close with the signature line word for
  word?

Any no means rewrite, not patch.

## Convicting, not condemning

"You already know what you are avoiding" moves someone. "You are behind and you should
feel it" gets a like and changes nothing. When a line sits near that border, it is
condemning. Rewrite it.

## Round against yourself

The panel reads 257.6 lb, so the spoken number is 258, never 257. Same rule for every
number on this account: a time rounds up, a rep count rounds down, a distance rounds down.
Never round in the direction that flatters him. It costs nothing and it is the difference
between a number people trust and a number people check.

## Banned moves

Fearmongering. Fake excitement. Corporate language. Forced slang. False urgency. Shame
as a motivator. Promises the training has not earned. Salesman energy in anything that
is not an actual sales context. Numbers that came from nowhere.

## Caption spec

One or two lines. Adds something the video did not say - the detail that got cut, the
question that invites a real answer. Never a summary of the video. Never a wall of
hashtags pretending to be a sentence.

Ask a question only when the answer is genuinely interesting to read. "Thoughts?" is
not a question.

## Hashtag spec

Three to five. Specific beats broad. A mix of the niche (calisthenics, weight loss
journey, ruck, marine pt) and the account's own tag so the library is findable. Skip
the giant generic ones that bury a small account.

## Cover frame spec

Pick the frame from inside the video, never a separate posed shot. Text on the cover is
three to five words and it is the number whenever there is a number. The cover is what
sells the video on a profile visit, and profile visits are how followers actually
happen.

## Pre post checklist

1. Hook readable in frame one with sound off
2. Every number traced to the tracker, no brackets left unfilled
3. Captions burned in and accurate
4. One clear action for the viewer
5. Correct CTA rung for the current follower stage, and only one ask
6. Signature line present if the video is motivational or identity, absent if it is not
7. Cover frame chosen and text set
8. Nothing claimed that did not happen
9. Filed at `content/scripts/YYYY-MM-DD-slug.md`, and logged in
   `content/performance-log.md` after it posts

## After posting

First hour: answer comments in words. The best comment becomes tomorrow's Answer Cut.

Log the numbers honestly at 24 hours and 7 days, including the posts that did nothing.
The flops are the most useful data the account has and the easiest to quietly not write
down.
