## 1. The premise is the asset

Most fitness challenge content has no stakes, so nobody follows it. Yours has three that people actually care about:

- **A signed contract.** 29 clauses. Reading them out loud is content by itself.
- **A tattoo.** The loser gets the other person's name permanently inked. That is the hook.
- **A rival you live with.** You're not competing against a number, you're competing against your girlfriend — and you have to do the outdoor hour with her.

Plus a hard end date with a guaranteed payoff. That last part matters more than it looks: **every new viewer has a reason to follow, because the resolution is in the future.** That's the whole retention engine of this series.

**The one-line pitch:** *"My girlfriend and I signed a contract. 100 days. Loser gets the winner's name tattooed on them."*

Lead with that for the first two weeks. Don't bury it.

## 2. The two-track system (read this before anything else)

The single most common way people fail a 100-day posting streak is trying to make all 100 posts good. By day 25 it's a chore, by day 40 they've missed one. At $100 a miss, that's expensive.

So split it into two tracks that do different jobs:

### Track A — the compliance post (daily, ~3 minutes, non-negotiable)

Its only job is to satisfy Clause 4 and protect you from the $100. Format-locked, zero decisions, zero pressure to be interesting. Story, static image, or a Strava screenshot is fine.

**Post it right after the outdoor hour, not at night.** The 11:59 PM deadline is the thing that's going to bite you on day 63 in an airport. Posting at 8 AM removes the deadline from your life entirely.

### Track B — the content post (3–4× per week, real effort)

This is what actually grows the account, feeds Date Miles Club, and turns into coaching clients. Pulled from the hook bank in §5. Track B posts also satisfy Clause 4 on the days they run — so on a Track B day you don't need a separate Track A post.

**Do not confuse the two.** Track A is insurance. Track B is growth.

### Daily template (Track A)

Matches the format the contract itself suggests, so it's compliant by construction:

```
DAY __ / 100
Workout: ✓ [what it was]
Outdoor hour: ✓ [distance / route]
Proof: attached
Mindset: [one line]
Biggest win: [one line]
Biggest challenge: [one line]
```

Attach the Strava screenshot and you're done.

## 3. The five phases

The calendar handed you a five-act structure. Use it — a 100-day series without an arc becomes a counter, and counters get boring around day 30.

### Phase 1 · THE CONTRACT — Days 1–10 (Sep 13–22)
Novelty is at its peak and so is your reach ceiling. Spend it establishing stakes, not showing workouts.

Post: the contract reveal, clause readings, baseline measurements, Day 1 photos, "why we did this," the tattoo clause in particular. End every video with *"follow so you see who loses."*

**Day 1 is the most important video of the entire 100 days.** Film it today. Structure: stakes in the first 2 seconds → show the signed contract → read Clause 7 (the tattoo) → baselines → "see you on day 100."

### Phase 2 · THE BUILD — Days 11–30 (Sep 23–Oct 12)
Novelty is gone, routine isn't formed yet. This is where the real personality shows up.

Post: the logistics nobody talks about (two hours a day has to come from somewhere), first near-misses, first arguments, early standings, how you're programming it.

### Phase 3 · THE MIDDLE — Days 31–60 (Oct 13–Nov 11)
Where series die. Needs the most variety and the most vulnerability.

Post: honest boredom, the grind, Halloween (Day 49), the halfway checkpoint (Day 50 — mid-challenge measurements, standings, what's changed). Lean on the couple dynamic and the coach angle here.

### Phase 4 · THE GAUNTLET — Days 61–85 (Nov 12 – Dec 6)
Cold, dark by 5:30, holidays, travel. The hardest stretch to *do* and the best stretch to *film* — this is peak relatability.

Post: Thanksgiving is **Day 75** (two hours before you eat — and 75 is the number that started all this, use that). Dark-and-cold outdoor hours, travel workarounds, holiday food vs. the contract.

### Phase 5 · THE VERDICT — Days 86–100 (Dec 7–21)
Tension. Someone is about to get a tattoo.

Post: countdown, final measurements, scoring, the mindset assessments, and the Day 100 finale on the winter solstice. **Day 100 should be the biggest video of the series** — final numbers, the verdict, and the tattoo appointment.

## 6. Systems — how not to lose $100

**Strava**
- Record every outdoor hour. The GPS record satisfies Clause 3 proof, and Strava's share image is a ready-made post asset.
- Name every activity `Day __/100 — ...`. Your Strava feed then *is* the accountability log from Clause 20, already timestamped and screenshot-ready.
- Turn on **privacy zones** around home and work. You're publishing a daily route, at a consistent time, publicly, for 100 days — that's a pattern worth obscuring. Clause 4 already says you don't have to reveal sensitive info.
- A Strava club for Date Miles Club makes the outdoor hours double as club activity.

**Never-miss insurance**
- Post Track A in the morning, right after the outdoor hour. Never let it reach the evening.
- Keep three pre-made emergency posts on your phone for travel/dead-battery days.
- Set a recurring 8 PM alarm as a backstop, not a deadline.
- Screenshot Strava the second the activity ends — proof and post asset in one action.
- Platform outage is the only excused miss (Clause 5). If one happens, screenshot it immediately.

**Batching**
- Film Track B content in 2 sessions a week, not daily. The outdoor hour is when you think of ideas; keep a note open.
- B-roll accumulates by itself — you're doing 200 hours of filmable activity. Capture 10 seconds every day even when you're not posting it.
